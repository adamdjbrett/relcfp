What makes something seem boring or unremarkable? What makes it worthy of attention? How do everyday actions shape how we live, think, and understand ourselves?

Syracuse University’s Religion Graduate Organization and Department of Religion invite proposals for **Mundane Religion**, their 2027 Graduate Student Conference. The conference asks how some aspects of everyday life come to stand out while others are taken for granted, and what studying ordinary habits can reveal about religion. It considers how the mundane can be something people seek to escape or a source of enchantment, and how ritual and practice punctuate daily life and domestic spaces.

- **Conference date** — Saturday, March 27, 2027
- **Location** — Syracuse University
- **Keynote speaker** — S. B. Rodriguez-Plate, Hamilton College
- **Proposal deadline** — January 15, 2027

## Suggested proposal topics

- Ritual, liturgy, habit, and repetition
- Ordinary time and the creation of religious calendars
- Acedia, tedium, ennui, boredom, apathy, and social malaise
- Attention and inattention in religious practice and thought
- Institutions, routinization, bureaucracy, and administrative labor
- Creation of archives, records, and documentation
- Hierarchies of labor and the differentiation of religious work
- Consumer economies, materiality, daily objects, and kitsch
- Popular religion and other devalued forms of religion
- The mundane in literature, art, and film
- Domestic life, caregiving, and maintenance
- Bodily discipline and ordinary gestures used in religious practice
- Methodological critiques of what gets overlooked or deemed uninteresting in the study of religion

## Submissions

[Submit a 300-word abstract through the online portal](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLScZaUhMcwz1zfKxcWs1QxSmXIJ9MSQt_30v2S44GGhnl72IMw/viewform?pli=1) by January 15, 2027. Panel submissions are strongly encouraged.
