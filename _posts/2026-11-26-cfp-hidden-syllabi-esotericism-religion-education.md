Kiegan Lloyd, of the Department of Educational Foundations in the College of Education at the University of Saskatchewan, is developing an edited collection, ***Hidden Syllabi: Esotericism, Religion and Education***, and invites expressions of interest from potential contributors.

The collection is built around a single question: **How do people learn what only some are permitted to know?** Lloyd is especially interested in the traces learners leave behind. A pencilled correction in a ritual notebook might show how an initiate learned to perform a ceremony. A student’s letter asking for the next lesson might reveal who decided they were ready to advance. The collection aims to bring together chapters that follow moments like these across different esoteric and religious communities.

## Background

The idea grew out of Lloyd’s work on two related book projects. *Selling Enlightenment: Occult Universities and the Entrepreneurial Spirit* is under a proposal-based contract with De Gruyter Brill for inclusion in the Aries Book Series. *Occult Pedagogies: Alternative Learning Communities in the Modern World* is under advanced consideration with Oxford University Press; Lloyd has submitted a full response to the reviewers and is awaiting word from the Global Acquisitions Editor for Education and Social Sciences on the editorial decision and next steps.

## Status of the project

*Hidden Syllabi* is under active consideration with Palgrave Macmillan and is proposed for the Palgrave Studies in New Religions and Alternative Spiritualities book series. Andrew Prashanth, Senior Editor for Religious Studies (Springer & Palgrave), suggested that Lloyd could invite expressions of interest now or wait for the series editor’s approval. The formal call for contributors is still being finalized, and Lloyd is waiting to hear from Palgrave about next steps. Lloyd is sharing the project in the meantime to learn who might be interested in contributing.

## Expressing interest
